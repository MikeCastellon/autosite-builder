# Writing guide

The reader is a local driver deciding whom to trust with their car. Write like the owner talking to a
neighbor: concrete, warm, short sentences, the business's own words where they gave them. Their style chips
set the tone (Bold & sporty: punchy; Luxury & high-end: calm and precise; Bright & friendly: upbeat; Classic
& trusted: plain and steady). Name the city or area where it helps search, not in every sentence.

Avoid filler that says nothing: "look no further", "elevate", "unparalleled", "top-notch", "second to none",
"one-stop shop", "we pride ourselves", "state-of-the-art". Avoid claims nobody gave (see SKILL.md).

## Google Business Profile

**Description** (250-750). Open with what they do and where ("Northside Shine Mobile Detailing brings car
detailing to your driveway across North Tampa, Lutz and Wesley Chapel."). Then the services by name, then
what sets them apart in the customer's own words (their "why us" and story answers), then how to book or get
in touch. No links, no prices or offers, no review quotes.

**Services.** The site's names, unchanged. Each description says what the work involves, in one or two
sentences, consistent with the owner's description. A tier name that says nothing (Gold, Deluxe) gets a
description only from the owner's or the site's words; never guess what a package includes.

**Categories.** Start from `plan.json`. The primary is the one that best describes the core business; add
only categories their services support (a detailer who never mentioned tint gets no tint category).

**Posts** (12, one a week). Each post has one idea and leads with it: Google shows the first line or two
before "More". 300-700 characters reads well; short paragraphs or a few dashes, no hashtags.

| Theme | What to write | Button |
|---|---|---|
| intro | Who they are and where, from their story and why-us answers | LEARN_MORE |
| service | One service: what the customer gets, who it suits, how to book | BOOK |
| work | One of their photos: what was done, from the photo and its note. Never guess the model, products or time taken | BOOK |
| tip | A general care tip for their trade (`plan.json` tip), useful on its own, then an invitation | LEARN_MORE |
| season | What the season does to cars where they are (no road salt in Florida), and their service that helps | BOOK |
| booking | How booking works on their site, only what the booking page offers | BOOK |
| area | The city or area they serve; mobile businesses come to the customer, shops give the area of the address | CALL (with a phone) |
| review | One pasted review quoted word for word in straight quotes, with the name as given, then a thank-you | LEARN_MORE |
| website | The new site is live: what visitors find there | LEARN_MORE |

Titles are short headlines (up to 58 characters), all different. Prices only as the owner wrote them, and
only in a service or work post. Opening hours only as given; never "open now" or "open 24/7".

**Image hints.** With a photo: describe what is visible ("The red coupe after its ceramic coating, side
view"). Without one: the photo they should take next ("A close-up of a clean wheel after a Full Detail").

## Search listing

- Title (up to 60): main service + area + the distinctive part of the name ("Mobile Detailing in North Tampa
  | Northside Shine"). Improve on `existing.metaTitle`; never stuff keywords.
- Description (110-160): the main services, the area and one reason from their answers.
- Keywords (4-12): what a nearby customer types: "ceramic coating wesley chapel", "mobile detailing lutz".

## Review requests

Same request for every customer, sent after the job. The text message (up to 300) and the email (subject up
to 80, body up to 1500) thank them, ask for an honest review on Google in one sentence, give the link (or
`[review link]`), and sign off with the business name. `[name]` greets them. Never offer anything for a
review, never ask for stars or a "positive" or "5-star" review, never only ask happy customers, never write
the review for them. See [google-rules.md](google-rules.md).

## Reply templates

One per rating, each ready to adapt (`[name]` for the reviewer). Don't mention the star count: the template
works for any review at that rating.

- 5 and 4: thank them by name, mention the kind of work in general terms, invite them back. 4 may ask what
  would have made it a better visit.
- 3: thank them for the honest feedback, ask what would have made it better, offer a way to talk.
- 2 and 1: apologize without arguing or blaming, no details of the job or the customer in public, invite them
  to call the business phone (only the one in the request) or reply, so it can be put right. Never offer a
  refund, discount or anything else in a public reply, and never ask them to change the review.

## Social

- Bio (up to 150): what, where, how to book. No link in the text (the profile's link field holds it).
- Captions (3): the launch of the new website; a service or a photo of their work; a review quote or a
  behind-the-scenes line from their story. Each different, 3-8 hashtags mixing service and place
  (#CeramicCoating #WesleyChapel), no hashtag stuffing.

## Notes

Short lines the admin acts on: "No Google place yet: the review texts have [review link].", "Only 3 photos:
posts 5, 9 and 11 ask for new ones.", "Their notes asked us to call them the #1 detailer; left out (no
source).", "Years in business not given: left out."
